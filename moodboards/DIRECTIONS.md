# Portfolio Redesign — Moodboard Directions

**Seed:** black & white, high contrast, play of light and shadow.
**Reference:** Joel Tjintjelaar — fine-art B&W long-exposure photography. Two poles of his work anchor our exploration: sculpted architectural darkness (towers "painted" with light against black skies) and long-exposure stillness (piers and seas dissolved into silver mist). A third direction pushes the seed somewhere he doesn't go: hard, graphic, noir light.

Each direction gets 8 generated images (mix of 4:5 portrait, 3:2 landscape, 1:1 square). All images: monochrome, photographic, no text, no people's faces.

---

## Direction 1 — MONOLITH
*Sculpted darkness. Architecture as tonal sculpture.*

The closest to Tjintjelaar's architectural work (his "B(l)ack to Black" ethos): buildings isolated against matte black skies, facades graded from deep shadow to luminous silver as if light were airbrushed onto them. Weight, mass, verticality, reverence. Silence with gravity.

**Visual vocabulary:** tonal gradients on monumental forms · matte black negative space · verticality · window-grid rhythms · light falling off into pure black · graphite and silver on velvet black.

**Portfolio translation (later):** near-black UI, huge editorial type emerging from darkness, gradients as section transitions, projects presented like monuments.

2. `Black and white fine art photograph, extreme low angle looking up at a curved concrete tower, soft light wrapping around the curve from one side into total darkness, dramatic tonal gradient, black sky, long exposure clouds streaked faintly, minimalist` — 4:5

## Direction 2 — KNIFE LIGHT
*Chiaroscuro. Hard light, hard edges, noir geometry.*

The counterpoint: where Monolith is soft gradients and mass, this is blades of light. Harsh sun through blinds, knife-edge shadow lines splitting frames into pure white and pure black, noir tension. Graphic, aggressive, cinematic. The light doesn't caress here — it cuts.

**Visual vocabulary:** raking shadows · venetian-blind slices · diagonal splits of white/black · silhouettes in doorways · shadow as a graphic object · crisp edges, no midtones wasted.

**Portfolio translation (later):** stark white/black blocks, hard diagonal layout splits, shadow-play hover states, type set like light through a window.

### Prompts
1. `Black and white photograph of harsh venetian blind shadows raking diagonally across a bare white wall, extreme contrast, hard midday light, film noir mood, crisp edges, minimalist` — 3:2
2. `High contrast monochrome photograph of a brutalist concrete stairwell in harsh sunlight, zigzag shadow cutting across the steps, pure white and pure black, graphic composition` — 4:5
3. `Black and white photograph of a grid of window light cast on the concrete floor of an empty gallery space, long sharp shadows, deep black surroundings, cinematic noir` — 3:2
4. `Monochrome photograph of a narrow alley between two buildings, one wall blazing white in sun, the other in pitch black shadow, knife-edge vertical line dividing the frame, abstract, high contrast` — 4:5
5. `Black and white photograph of a doorway in a dark room with a single hard beam of light entering at an angle, geometric light pool on the floor, everything else absolute black, dramatic chiaroscuro` — 4:5
6. `High contrast black and white photograph of rooftop chimneys and vents casting long hard shadows at a steep angle, abstract graphic composition, pure blacks and whites, noir` — 1:1
7. `Black and white abstract photograph, a concrete edge splitting the frame diagonally into a pure white half and a pure black half, tiny texture detail along the edge, extreme minimalism, high contrast` — 1:1
8. `Monochrome photograph of a spiral fire escape casting intricate hard shadows on a white brick wall, harsh sun, graphic pattern, film noir energy, high contrast` — 4:5

## Direction 3 — LONG STILL
*Long-exposure atmosphere. Silver mist, horizons, breath.*

Tjintjelaar's seascape pole: minutes-long exposures that turn water to glass and sky to fog, dark man-made structures standing alone in vast pale emptiness. Where Monolith is heavy and Knife Light is sharp, this is weightless. Almost-white greys, one black anchor, enormous negative space. Calm confidence.

**Visual vocabulary:** horizon bands · fog and mist gradients · glassy long-exposure water · lone dark structures in white space · silver-on-white with a single black accent · horizontal rhythm.

**Portfolio translation (later):** paper-white UI, vast whitespace, thin quiet type, slow fades, work floating like objects in fog.

### Prompts
1. `Fine art black and white long exposure photograph of a wooden pier receding into a misty sea, water smoothed to glass, sky and sea merging into silver fog, pier posts in deep black, minimalist, ethereal` — 3:2
2. `Black and white long exposure seascape, a row of dark wooden groyne posts marching into flat white water, dense fog, near-white sky, extreme minimalism, fine art print quality` — 3:2
3. `Monochrome fine art photograph, pure horizon study: three soft tonal bands of pale sea, mist and sky, no objects, meditative gradient, long exposure smoothness` — 3:2
4. `Black and white photograph of a lighthouse half-dissolved in fog, only its dark silhouette and base visible, vast empty white space around it, long exposure, minimalist fine art` — 4:5
5. `Fine art black and white long exposure of a single dark rock in a smooth silver sea, two minute exposure, water like liquid glass, soft gradient sky, profound stillness` — 1:1
6. `Minimalist monochrome seascape, a tiny black cargo ship on the horizon of a vast pale silver sea, enormous empty sky, long exposure, fine art, breathtaking negative space` — 3:2
7. `Black and white long exposure photograph of an abandoned sea fort structure on stilts standing in misty water, floating between white sea and white sky, dark silhouette, fine art minimalism` — 4:5
8. `Monochrome photograph of tidal ripple patterns in wet sand at low tide fading into fog in the distance, soft silver light, texture dissolving into nothing, fine art minimal seascape` — 1:1

## Process
1. Connect FLORA MCP → generate all 24 images.
2. Assemble three moodboard pages (one per direction) for side-by-side review.
3. Peter picks a direction (or a hybrid) → that board becomes the design seed for the actual portfolio.
